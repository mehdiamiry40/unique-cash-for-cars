# Can you sell a car without a roadworthy in Queensland?

<PostDetails slug="sell-car-without-roadworthy-qld" />

Yes, in some situations. Queensland calls a roadworthy a **safety certificate**. You do not need one merely to advertise a registered car, to sell a vehicle that is already unregistered, or to trade a registered vehicle directly to a licensed motor dealer. A normal private sale of a registered light vehicle generally does require a current certificate before the vehicle is disposed of.

The registration status and buyer matter more than whether the car starts. A damaged car does not automatically become exempt, and a business calling itself a recycler, wrecker or cash-for-cars buyer is not automatically a licensed motor dealer.

**This guide is general information, not legal advice.** Rules and forms change. Check the current [Queensland Transport and Main Roads safety-certificate guidance](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/roadworthy) for your circumstances before completing a sale.

## The quick answer for each type of sale

- **Registered car sold privately:** the seller generally needs a current safety certificate before disposal and must give the certificate, copy or electronic certificate number to the buyer.
- **Registered car traded to a licensed motor dealer:** no safety certificate is required for that trade.
- **Unregistered car:** it can be sold without a safety certificate, but it cannot simply be driven on public roads.
- **Car sold for parts:** a registered vehicle must first be de-registered.
- **Damaged registered car:** damage does not create a blanket exemption. Repair it, use a valid dealer-sale path, or cancel the registration and sell it unregistered.
- **Written-off car:** separate notification and inspection rules may apply in addition to the registration and sale process.

There are limited exemptions for particular circumstances, including some estate and spouse transfers and vehicles with a garage address in an exempt area. If one may apply, confirm it with TMR rather than relying on a general summary.

## A roadworthy is officially a safety certificate

The Queensland safety-certificate inspection checks minimum safety standards for items such as tyres, brakes, steering, suspension, rust or body damage, the windscreen and lights. It is not a comprehensive mechanical inspection and does not guarantee how long the engine, transmission or other components will last.

Only an approved inspection station can issue the certificate. For a private seller, a certificate is generally valid for two months or 2,000 km, whichever comes first, and it can be used for one transfer only. A significant change in the vehicle's condition can also end its usefulness, so do not assume an older certificate remains valid after new damage.

## Selling a registered car privately

You may advertise the vehicle before it has a certificate. The certificate is needed before the ordinary private disposal, not before the listing goes online.

The seller is responsible for obtaining it and giving the buyer the original handwritten certificate, a digital copy of an electronic certificate, or its electronic certificate number. The buyer cannot complete the standard transfer without a current certificate, and the seller can remain exposed to tolls and infringements while the registration stays in their name.

Queensland requires the registration transfer process to be completed within 14 days. If the vehicle has an LPG or another gas fuel system, a separate gas certificate may also be required. Our [Queensland registration-transfer guide](/transferring-car-registration-in-queensland) explains the seller and buyer steps.

## Selling an unregistered car without a roadworthy

An [unregistered vehicle can be sold in Queensland without a safety certificate](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/buying/unregistered/selling). Create a clear paper trail that both parties keep. TMR recommends recording:

- the seller's and buyer's signatures
- the sale date
- the make and model
- the VIN, chassis number or engine number

The exemption from a safety certificate is not permission to drive an unsafe or unregistered car. A road journey may require an unregistered vehicle permit and journey-specific CTP insurance. If the car is unsafe to drive, arrange a tow truck or transporter instead.

If the car is still registered but the intended sale is unregistered, cancel the registration and deal with the plates first. Use our checklist for [cancelling Queensland registration after a sale](/cancel-car-registration-queensland-after-sale) and retain TMR's confirmation with the signed receipt.

## Selling a registered car to a licensed motor dealer

Queensland's dealer exemption means a safety certificate is not required when the registered vehicle is traded to a licensed motor dealer. The important word is **licensed**.

Confirm the licence belongs to the legal entity acquiring the car. A website description or collection truck does not prove that a recycler, dismantler or cash-for-cars operator qualifies for the exemption. If the buyer is not a licensed motor dealer, use the sale path that matches the vehicle's registration status rather than assuming collection makes the roadworthy requirement disappear.

## What if the car is damaged or cannot pass?

TMR's [used-vehicle selling guidance](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/buying/rules/selling) identifies four practical options for an unsafe or defective registered vehicle: repair the defects, sell it to an auto dealer, sell it to an auto-parts dismantler, or sell it unregistered.

That does not mean every dismantler purchase uses the licensed-dealer exemption. If the transaction is a sale for parts, TMR says the vehicle must be de-registered first. Cancel the registration, surrender or retain the plates as TMR directs, record the sale and arrange safe transport. Do not drive a defective vehicle merely to complete the handover.

Compare the repair cost with the car's realistic value before choosing. Our guide to [damaged-car options on the Gold Coast](/what-to-do-with-a-damaged-car-on-the-gold-coast-a-complete-guide) covers repair, insurance, parts and whole-vehicle sale decisions.

## Written-off vehicles have extra rules

A failed safety inspection is not the same as a formal write-off. If an insurer or another qualified notifier has classified the vehicle, check its written-off status and confirm whether TMR has already been notified before disposal.

A **repairable write-off** may be eligible for Queensland registration after the required repairs, a safety-certificate inspection and a written-off vehicle inspection. Its written-off history remains recorded. A **statutory write-off** can never be re-registered in Australia.

Where an owner must notify TMR of a total loss, that notification is required before selling or disposing of the notifiable vehicle or an identifying-number part. Read TMR's current [written-off vehicle notification guidance](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/vehicle-safety/written-off-vehicles/notify) and our explanation of [statutory and repairable write-offs](/statutory-vs-repairable-write-off-queensland) before arranging collection.

## Checklist before you accept an offer

1. Confirm whether the vehicle is registered, unregistered or formally written off.
2. Decide whether the transaction is a private registered transfer, licensed-dealer trade or unregistered sale.
3. If relying on the dealer exemption, verify the buyer's motor-dealer licence.
4. Obtain the safety certificate, or cancel registration, according to that sale path.
5. Record the buyer, seller, date, amount, VIN and vehicle details in a signed receipt.
6. Complete the transfer, cancellation and any written-off notification on time.
7. Arrange a permit or professional transport instead of driving an unsafe vehicle.
