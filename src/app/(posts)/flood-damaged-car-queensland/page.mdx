# Flood damaged car in Queensland: what to do before you claim, repair or sell

<PostDetails slug="flood-damaged-car-queensland" />

Do not start it. That is the most useful thing to know about a flood damaged car, and it comes before insurance, repair quotes or offers. RACQ's advice on [what to do if your vehicle has been flooded](https://www.racq.com.au/articles/driving-tips/what-to-do-if-your-vehicle-has-been-flooded) is not to start or drive a car if water has reached the interior, the engine bay or the exhaust. Turning the key can be what turns a wet car into a wrecked engine.

What happens next depends on two things you can find out within a few days: whether the car is insured for flood, and how far the water came up inside it. Those two answers decide whether this is a repair, a write-off or a straight sale.

**This guide is general information, not legal advice.** Written-off vehicle rules are administered by Queensland Transport and Main Roads and they change. Check TMR's current [written-off vehicle guidance](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/vehicle-safety/written-off-vehicles/about) and your insurer's product disclosure statement for your own situation.

## Which situation are you in?

- **Insured, and you have not claimed yet:** stop here and read the next two sections. Lodge the claim before you move, clean or sell anything.
- **Insured, and the insurer has declared a total loss:** the car is now a repairable or statutory write-off. That classification decides what it can ever be used for.
- **Insured, and the insurer will repair it:** the claim runs its course. Keep every report, because a future buyer may ask about the flood.
- **Not insured, or insured without flood cover:** nobody will assess it for you. Get a mechanic's written report, then decide.
- **A hybrid or electric car that sat in water:** treat it as a hazard until someone qualified has inspected it.

## The first few days after the water goes down

**Photograph it where it sits.** RACQ suggests photos of the outside and the inside, through the windows if need be, and a clear shot of the **waterline**, the tide mark the water leaves on the upholstery, door trims and panels. That line is the single most useful piece of evidence an assessor will look for, and it starts to fade once the car is dried or cleaned.

**Leave the key out of the ignition.** A running engine draws air through its intake. If water is sitting there, the engine pulls the water in instead, and because water does not compress the way air and fuel do, the pistons and connecting rods take the force. RACQ warns that flooded hybrid and electric vehicles add a risk of electric shock or fire from short circuits, so the high-voltage system is a job for someone trained in it, not a driveway check. Our [hybrid battery guide](/dead-hybrid-battery-repair-or-sell) covers what that system is worth once it has been assessed.

**Have it towed, not driven.** Even if it does start, water may have got into the fuel, the oil, the transmission and the wiring. Driving it to a repairer turns an assessment question into a mechanical failure.

**Tell whoever tows it exactly where it is.** A car in an apartment basement in Surfers Paradise or Broadbeach needs the clearance height and parking level passed on before a truck is sent. A car left on soft, silt-covered ground beside a canal or the Nerang River may need a different loading setup from a kerbside job.

## How the insurer decides on a flood damaged car

If you are covered, the insurer's assessor inspects the car and makes two decisions. The first is whether it is repairable at all. The second is whether repairing it makes financial sense against what the car is worth.

The water level inside the cabin carries most of the weight. A car that took water across the floor for an hour is a different proposition from one that sat submerged to the dash for two days. Whether the water was fresh, or the brackish and salt water that comes up through tidal canals and the Broadwater, also matters: salt is far harder on wiring, connectors and steel. Seats, carpet and sound deadening hold water for a long time, and modern cars route modules and wiring looms under the seats and through the floor.

If the assessor declares a total loss, the car is classified as one of two things:

- **Repairable write-off:** it can be repaired and returned to the road, but only after it passes both a written-off vehicle inspection and a safety-certificate inspection. TMR sets out both on its [written-off vehicle inspections page](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/vehicle-safety/written-off-vehicles/inspections).
- **Statutory write-off:** it can never be registered again anywhere in Australia, even if repaired. From that point it is parts and metal.

Either way the record stays with the VIN for good. Our guide to [statutory and repairable write-offs](/statutory-vs-repairable-write-off-queensland) explains both classifications in detail, including the choice between a full payout and keeping the wreck for a reduced one.

Insurance cover for flood is not universal. Read the product disclosure statement or ask the insurer directly how the policy treats flood, storm surge and water entering the car, rather than assuming comprehensive cover means flood is included.

## When the car is not insured

Without an insurer there is no assessor, no classification and no payout. That leaves you making the repair decision yourself, so make it from a written report rather than a hopeful start-up.

RACQ's advice for uninsured owners is to contact their mechanic. Ask them to check the engine oil, the transmission fluid and the fuel for water, look under the carpets and seats, and test the electrics, then put the findings and a quote in writing. The quote is only half the comparison. The other half is what the car would be worth once repaired, knowing it carries a flood history you will have to disclose.

If the damage makes the car a total loss, there is one rule to check before anything leaves your hands. TMR says that if a vehicle is a total write-off and no insurer or other notifier has already written it off, the owner must notify TMR before selling or disposing of it. Read TMR's [written-off vehicle notification guidance](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/vehicle-safety/written-off-vehicles/notify) to work out whether that applies to your car.

## Repair it or sell it?

Repair is more likely to make sense when the water stayed low, it was fresh, the car was moved and dried quickly, and the rest of the car is in good order. It is much harder to justify when water reached the dashboard, the car sat in it for days, or the electrics are already misbehaving.

The problem with flood damage is that it rarely finishes when the car is dried out. The PPSR's guidance on [storm-damaged vehicles](https://www.ppsr.gov.au/newsroom/storm-damage) warns that corrosion of electrical and other components can develop over time and not show up for years. A repair quote prices what the mechanic can see today. It cannot price the connector that corrodes in eighteen months.

Weigh three numbers: the written repair quote, what the car will be worth afterwards with its flood history, and what it is worth now as it stands. What you paid for it does not belong in the sum.

## Selling a flood damaged car honestly

Be upfront about the flood, in writing. Queensland's Office of Fair Trading says businesses selling [water-damaged goods, including vehicles](https://www.qld.gov.au/law/laws-regulated-industries-and-accountability/queensland-laws-and-regulations/fair-trading-services-programs-and-resources/fair-trading-latest-news/disaster-assistance/water-damaged-products), must make the water damage clear and must not mislead buyers about the history. A private sale is a different legal setting, but hiding it gains little. Written-off status shows on a [PPSR vehicle search](https://www.ppsr.gov.au/searching/do-used-car-or-vehicle-search), and a buyer who discovers a waterline under the carpet later has a reason to come back to you.

The sale path then follows the car's status:

- **Statutory write-off:** it can only go for parts or scrap. Make sure any notification TMR requires has been made first.
- **Repairable write-off:** it can be sold as a repair project or for parts. The buyer, not you, carries the inspections if they want it back on the road.
- **Still registered and not written off:** an ordinary private sale generally needs a current safety certificate, which a flooded car is unlikely to pass. The alternatives are covered in our guide to [selling a car without a roadworthy in Queensland](/sell-car-without-roadworthy-qld).
- **Leaving the road for good:** cancel the registration and deal with the plates. Our checklist for [cancelling Queensland registration](/cancel-car-registration-queensland-after-sale) walks through it.

TMR says an [unregistered vehicle can be sold](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/buying/unregistered/selling) without a safety certificate. Record both parties' signatures, the date, the make and model and the VIN on a receipt, and add a line stating the car was flood damaged. Both of you keep a copy.

## What a flooded car is still worth

Less than a dry one, but rarely nothing. Body panels, glass, wheels, tyres and some mechanical parts can come through a flood intact, and the metal in the car has value whatever happened to the wiring. Interior trim and electronics are usually the first things lost.

The figure depends on the make, model and year, how high the water went, whether it was salt or fresh, how long the car has sat since, and whether it is complete. A car that is left wet in a garage keeps deteriorating, so once the insurer has finished with it, or you have your mechanic's report, make the decision rather than letting it sit.
