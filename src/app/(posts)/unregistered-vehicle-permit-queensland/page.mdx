# Do you need an unregistered vehicle permit in Queensland?

<PostDetails slug="unregistered-vehicle-permit-queensland" />

Only if the car is going to be driven. An unregistered vehicle permit authorises one journey, on a road, in a vehicle that is not currently registered. Put the same car on a tow truck, a tilt tray or a trailer and nothing is being driven, so no permit is needed for it.

That distinction decides the whole question, and it catches people out in a specific way: the vehicles whose owners most want a permit are often the exact vehicles that cannot be issued one. Transport and Main Roads will not grant a permit for a car that is not in a safe condition to drive. The flat-batteried sedan under a Labrador carport, the ute with a failed inspection sitting at a repairer, the hail-damaged hatch nobody has started since March — a permit is not the answer for any of them.

**This guide is general information, not legal advice.** Permit conditions, fees and eligibility change. Check TMR's current [unregistered vehicle permit page](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/buying/unregistered/uvp) for your circumstances before you plan a journey.

## What the permit is actually for

A permit covers a nominated journey for a nominated purpose — getting a dormant car to a safety-certificate inspection, to a repairer, to a customer service centre to be registered, or to a new address. It is not a short-term registration and it is not a loophole for driving a car you have let lapse.

Three conditions travel with it:

- **The route.** The permit is issued for the time the journey takes, and the journey must follow the most direct route to the destination. A detour to a mate's place at Nerang on the way is outside what you paid for.
- **The duration.** Permits are issued for one to seven days. The permit runs from 12.01am on the first day and expires at midnight on the last.
- **The document.** The driver has to carry the permit for the journey.

Refunds are limited. An expired permit is not refundable at all, and where a refund is available for an unexpired portion, it applies only to the part beyond one day — the first day is never refunded. Book the dates you actually need rather than padding them.

## The eligibility rules that rule cars out

This is the part worth reading before anything else, because five separate situations stop a permit being issued. TMR will not issue one where the vehicle:

- is not in a safe condition to drive, or would not pass — or has already failed — a safety inspection
- still has number plates attached, including personalised or customised plates
- is being moved into Queensland from interstate
- has no VIN or chassis number
- is subject to a serious unresolved manufacturer defect recall

The first two account for most refusals. **Safe condition to drive** is assessed against the same standards as a [Queensland safety certificate](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/roadworthy) inspection — brakes, tyres, steering, suspension, lights, windscreen, structural rust. A car that has already failed one cannot be permitted around that failure, and our guide on [selling a car without a roadworthy in Queensland](/sell-car-without-roadworthy-qld) covers the sale paths that open up instead.

The plates rule surprises people. A car with expired registration usually still has its plates bolted on, and that alone makes it ineligible until they come off. They must stay detached while the vehicle is driven under the permit, and go back on once it has passed inspection or the registration is renewed. If the plates are personalised, deal with them deliberately rather than leaving them in the boot — they are a separate registration product with their own rules.

## Class 22 CTP is a second purchase

The permit is not insurance. Driving an unregistered vehicle on a Queensland road also requires **Class 22 compulsory third party insurance**, bought from one of the state's licensed CTP insurers, and its premium is separate from the permit fee. The Motor Accident Insurance Commission explains [CTP for unregistered vehicles](https://maic.qld.gov.au/for-drivers/ctp-insurance-for-unregistered-vehicles/) and what the class covers.

Match the dates. The CTP certificate and the permit should cover the same days, because cover that starts a day late leaves the first leg of the journey uninsured — on a road, in a car with no plates. Class 22 is priced as a base rate for the first day plus a daily rate after that, so a seven-day permit costs more on both lines than a one-day permit.

## How to apply

You can apply [online through TMR](https://www.service.transport.qld.gov.au/unregisteredvehiclepermit/) or in person at a transport and motoring customer service centre. The online path needs a Queensland customer reference number — your licence number will usually do it.

One practical difference between the two: the online service does not accept a prepaid Class 22 certificate, though you can nominate the CTP insurer of your choice while you buy the permit. If you have already paid for a certificate, take it to a counter instead. A permit also has to be bought in the state or territory where the journey starts, so an interstate collection is the other jurisdiction's paperwork, not Queensland's.

## Towing sidesteps all of it

Where a tow truck tows an unregistered vehicle, [TMR does not require an unregistered vehicle permit](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/buying/unregistered/selling) for the towed car. The tow truck's rear number plate is removed and fixed to the rear of the unregistered vehicle, or an accessory plate issued to the tow truck is used instead. No permit fee, no Class 22 premium, no safety-condition test to satisfy.

For a car that is not going back on the road, that is almost always the cheaper and simpler path. It is also the only lawful one when the car will not roll, will not steer, has seized brakes or is missing wheels — conditions no permit can authorise a driver to take onto the M1.

Access is what changes a tow booking on the Gold Coast, not distance. An apartment basement at Surfers Paradise with a signed clearance height, a blocked-in car in a Southport unit block, soft ground on acreage behind Mudgeeraba and a kerbside job at Mermaid Waters each need a different loading setup. Give whoever is quoting the suburb, the parking level, the clearance height and whether the car rolls and steers.

## If the car is not going back on the road

A permit is a bridge to registration. When there is no registration at the other end, the questions change: what to do with the plates, whether the registration is cancelled or has simply expired, and whether any refund is owing.

Work that out before the car moves. ## A short decision path

1. Decide whether the car will be **driven** or **transported**. Transported needs no permit.
2. If driven, assess honestly whether it would pass a safety inspection today. If not, stop — arrange transport.
3. Check the other disqualifiers: plates attached, interstate move, missing VIN, unresolved recall.
4. Work out the real journey and the most direct route, then the minimum number of days.
5. Buy Class 22 CTP for exactly those dates from a licensed insurer.
6. Apply online with your CRN, or at a customer service centre if you hold a prepaid certificate.
7. Detach the plates, carry the permit, and drive the route you nominated.
8. If the destination is a new owner rather than an inspection, confirm the [registration transfer steps](/transferring-car-registration-in-queensland) before the handover.

The honest summary: an unregistered vehicle permit in Queensland is a good tool for a sound car with lapsed registration, and the wrong tool for a car that stopped being sound some time ago.
