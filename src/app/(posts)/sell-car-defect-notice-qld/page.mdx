# Selling a car with a defect notice in Queensland: clear it, cancel it or sell to a dealer

<PostDetails slug="sell-car-defect-notice-qld" />

Yes, you can sell a car with a defect notice in Queensland, but handing over the keys does not end the notice. The obligation to fix the car sits with you as the owner until one of three things happens: the defects are repaired and the notice is cleared, the registration is cancelled, or the car is disposed of to a licensed motor dealer. The last two only release you if you also send written notice within 7 days.

Most of the trouble comes from the fourth path people try first — a quick private sale with the notice unmentioned. It rarely works, because a registered car sold privately still needs a safety certificate, and a car with uncleared defects is the car least likely to get one.

**This guide is general information, not legal advice.** Defect notice rules, clearance methods and forms change. Read TMR's current [defect notice guidance](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/buying/vehicleinspection/randominspection/defect) and the conditions printed on your own notice before you decide.

## What a defect notice actually is

A **defect notice** is not a fine. It is a written notice, usually issued after a police or TMR roadside inspection, requiring the owner of a light vehicle to have it repaired so it is safe to drive. It states what is defective, what has to be done and how the notice is to be cleared. An on-the-spot fine can be issued on the same stop, but that is a separate document with a separate deadline.

Queensland uses three kinds of notice, and the kind you hold changes what you can do with the car this week:

- **Major defect notice.** Issued when using the vehicle on a road poses an imminent and serious safety risk. The car may be ordered off the road on the spot, which means it cannot be driven away from where it was stopped. A tow truck or trailer moves it.
- **Minor defect notice.** Issued when there is a safety risk that is not imminent and serious. The notice will usually allow the car to be driven under stated conditions — to your home or a repairer, for example — until it is cleared.
- **Self-clearing defect notice.** Issued when the defect does not pose a safety risk, or the number plate is obscured or illegible. You fix it by the date on the notice, and no inspection is normally needed to clear it.

The officer may also attach a **defective vehicle label** to the car. Under Queensland's [vehicle standards and safety regulation](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/sl-2021-0114), you must not remove it without a reasonable excuse. Peeling it off before photos for a listing is not one.

## Ignoring it is the expensive option

Selling around a notice is tempting when the repair looks dear. The consequences of not complying are set out by TMR: an on-the-spot fine, an order to present the vehicle for a full inspection, or action to cancel the registration. None of those stop because the car is now parked at someone else's house, and while the registration stays in your name you can also remain liable for tolls and infringements on it.

The honest choice is between the three exits below. Each suits a different car.

## Exit one: repair the defects and clear the notice

Repairing makes sense when the defects are small against the car's value — a cracked windscreen, worn tyres, a blown headlight globe, an exhaust leak. The notice tells you how it must be cleared. For many notices that is an inspection at an **approved inspection station**, which can record the clearance electronically so you do not have to visit a transport and motoring customer service centre. Where the station cannot do that, the examiner signs the back of the paper notice and you present it to TMR yourself.

Once cleared, the car is an ordinary registered vehicle again. A private sale then follows the normal rules: a current [Queensland safety certificate](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/roadworthy) before disposal, given to the buyer, and the transfer lodged on time. Our guide to [selling a car without a roadworthy in Queensland](/sell-car-without-roadworthy-qld) sets out when that certificate is and is not required.

Get a written quote before you commit. A defect notice on an older car often lists one fault, and the inspection to clear it finds three more. If the repair quote is heading towards what the car is worth, stop and look at the other two exits.

## Exit two: cancel the registration

Cancelling the registration is the cleanest exit for a car that is not going back on the road — one with structural rust, a failed engine, or defects that would cost more than the car. Under the regulation you need not comply with the defect notice if the registration is cancelled **and** you give written notice of the cancellation to the officer who issued the notice within 7 days.

That second step is the one people miss. Cancelling the rego and assuming TMR's systems connect it to the notice is not the same as telling the issuing officer in writing. Keep a copy of what you sent and the date.

Once the registration is cancelled, the car is an unregistered vehicle. It can be [sold unregistered](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/buying/unregistered/selling) without a safety certificate, to a private buyer, a dismantler or a car buyer, with a signed receipt recording the date, the make and model, and the VIN. Our checklist for [cancelling Queensland registration](/cancel-car-registration-queensland-after-sale) covers surrendering the plates and claiming any refund owing.

## Exit three: dispose of it to a licensed motor dealer

TMR's [used-vehicle selling guidance](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/buying/rules/selling) lists selling to a dealer as one of the options for a defective vehicle, and the regulation releases you from the notice if the car is disposed of to a dealer and you give TMR written notice of the dealer's name and address within 7 days.

The key word is **licensed**. A tow truck, a recycling yard and a cash-for-cars website do not each prove a motor-dealer licence. Ask for the licence number, check it against the Queensland register, and confirm it belongs to the legal entity actually buying the car rather than to a related business. Unique Cash For Cars is operated by A Plus Car Removal Pty Ltd, and the operator and licence details are on our [company details page](/about) for you to verify before deciding how to complete the sale.

Record the dealer's legal name and address on the receipt at handover. You need both for the written notice, and chasing them a week later is harder than writing them down in the driveway.

## Moving a defected car without driving it

A sale is rarely one of the journeys a defect notice allows. Read the driving conditions on your notice: a car ordered off the road cannot be driven anywhere, and a defective vehicle must not be driven or parked on a road unless it is safe and the notice's conditions are being followed.

If the car was defected on the M1 or the Smith Street Motorway and towed home, it will need to be towed again. The same goes for a car sitting on a kerbside in Southport with a major defect label on the windscreen — moving it to the buyer's place is not an allowed trip. An unregistered vehicle permit does not fix this either; TMR will not issue one for a vehicle that is not in a safe condition to drive, as our guide to the [unregistered vehicle permit](/unregistered-vehicle-permit-queensland) explains.

For whoever is collecting, describe the access as well as the car: a basement car park in Surfers Paradise with a clearance height, a battle-axe driveway in Nerang, a car blocked in at a Labrador unit block. Those details decide the tow setup more than the defect does.

## Selling a car with a defect notice: which exit fits

1. **Defects are minor and the car is worth keeping on the road:** repair, clear the notice, then sell with a safety certificate.
2. **The repair costs more than the car is worth to you:** cancel the registration, give written notice to the issuing officer within 7 days, then sell it unregistered.
3. **You are selling to a business:** confirm it holds a current motor-dealer licence in the buyer's own name, then give TMR written notice of the dealer's name and address within 7 days.
4. **The car is ordered off the road or unsafe:** whichever exit you choose, arrange a tow rather than a drive.

Whatever the path, tell the buyer about the defect notice in writing and keep copies of the notice, the receipt, and every written notice you sent. Value is driven by the same things as any other car — make, model, year, condition, completeness and demand for its parts — and an honest description of the defects is what lets a buyer price it once rather than twice.
