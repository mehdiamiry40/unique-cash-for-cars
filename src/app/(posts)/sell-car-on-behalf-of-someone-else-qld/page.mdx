# Selling a car on behalf of someone else in Queensland: the authority you need first

<PostDetails slug="sell-car-on-behalf-of-someone-else-qld" />

Yes, provided you can show you are allowed to. When you are selling a car on behalf of someone else in Queensland, the car does not become yours to sell because you hold the keys, pay the insurance or are family. The registered owner still owns it. What changes is who signs, and the signature only counts if it comes with written authority that Transport and Main Roads, the buyer and any finance company will accept.

The kind of authority depends on one question: can the owner still make their own decisions? If they can, they either sign themselves or authorise you in writing. If they cannot, you need an enduring power of attorney that is already in effect, or a tribunal appointment. Everything else in this guide follows from that split.

**This guide is general information, not legal advice.** Registration and substitute decision-making rules change, and a disputed or complicated situation needs a solicitor. Check TMR's current guidance on [transferring registration in person](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/transfer/rego) and the Queensland Government's [power of attorney information](https://www.qld.gov.au/law/legal-mediation-and-justice-of-the-peace/power-of-attorney-and-making-decisions-for-others/power-of-attorney) before you sign anything.

If the owner has died, this is the wrong guide. A death puts a TMR restriction on the registration and brings in executors and next of kin; our guide to [selling a deceased estate car in Queensland](/deceased-estate-car-sale-queensland) covers that process.

## Which situation are you in?

- **The owner can decide but cannot attend:** they are overseas, working away, in hospital for a stretch, or simply do not want to deal with it. They sign a written authority for you to act on the registration. This is the simplest case.
- **The owner has lost capacity and made an enduring power of attorney:** the attorney for financial matters can sell, as long as the power has started and the document does not exclude it.
- **The owner has lost capacity and there is no enduring power of attorney:** nobody can sign yet. QCAT may need to appoint an administrator first.
- **You hold only a general power of attorney:** it works while the owner has capacity and ends when they lose it.
- **The car is registered to a business:** the business authorises the person acting for it, and the individual's own say-so is not enough.
- **You own the car but it was never transferred into your name:** that is a registration problem, not an authority problem. Our guide to [transferring car registration in Queensland](/transferring-car-registration-in-queensland) covers it.

## When the owner can still decide

If the owner has capacity, the cleanest option is for them to sign the transfer paperwork themselves, even if you handle everything else. A Queensland registration transfer uses the [vehicle registration transfer application, form F3520](https://www.support.transport.qld.gov.au/qt/formsdat.nsf/Forms/QF3520), which both buyer and seller sign. The buyer lodges Part A and the seller keeps Part B.

When the owner cannot be there, TMR provides an **authority to represent a registered operator**, [form F5225](https://www.support.transport.qld.gov.au/qt/formsdat.nsf/Forms/QF5225). It lets the owner authorise a named individual to sign and present registration forms for them. On TMR's in-person transfer page, the representative presents the original authority and evidence of their own identity, and completes the authorisation question on the F3520. Read the current form before relying on it. It sets out whose identity documents the representative needs and how long the authority lasts.

Two practical points. TMR wants the **original** signed authority, so a photo of it on your phone will not do. And the authority covers registration dealings; it does not settle whether the owner has agreed to a particular buyer or price. Get that in writing too, even a short signed note naming the car, the buyer and the figure, so nobody is surprised later.

## When the owner has lost capacity

This is where most of the trouble starts. A parent moves into aged care, the car sits in the garage at Helensvale, and the family assume the eldest child can simply sell it. They can only if the paperwork was done while the parent could still do it.

A **general power of attorney** ends when the person who made it loses capacity. It is useful for the overseas-owner case above, but it is worthless once dementia or a serious brain injury is involved.

An **enduring power of attorney** is built for exactly this. For financial matters, the owner chose when the attorney's power starts: immediately, on a set date, or when they lose capacity. If they lose capacity before the chosen start, the attorney's power still operates during that period. Read the document itself before acting; some restrict what the attorney may do, and TMR and buyers will want to see it. The [Office of the Public Guardian](https://www.publicguardian.qld.gov.au/making-decisions/future-planning/your-role-and-responsibilities-as-an-attorney) sets out what an attorney is and is not allowed to do.

If there is no enduring power of attorney, nobody in the family has authority by default, however close they are. [QCAT can appoint an administrator](https://www.qcat.qld.gov.au/case-types/decision-making-for-adults-with-impaired-capacity/administrators) to make financial decisions for an adult with impaired capacity. That takes time. Until the appointment is made, leave the car where it is rather than letting someone sell it informally.

## The rules an attorney sells under

Holding the power is only half of it. An attorney has to act in the owner's interests, and a few things follow directly from that.

- **You cannot quietly buy it yourself.** Buying the principal's car for yourself is a **conflict transaction**. It is allowed only if the enduring power of attorney document authorises it or QCAT approves it.
- **The money is the owner's.** Keep the owner's money separate from your own. The sale price goes to an account in the owner's name, not to yours for safekeeping.
- **Keep the records.** Hold on to the offer, the receipt and the bank record of the payment. An attorney can be asked to account for what they did.

None of this makes a sale harder. It just means the sale needs a paper trail good enough to show someone else later.

## Registration, roadworthy and finance still apply

Selling a car on behalf of someone else does not create an exemption from anything. A registered light vehicle sold privately generally still needs a current [safety certificate](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/roadworthy). The alternatives are the same as for any seller: trade it to a licensed motor dealer, sell it unregistered, or de-register it for a sale for parts. Our guide to [selling a car without a roadworthy in Queensland](/sell-car-without-roadworthy-qld) sets out which path needs what.

Finance can also sit behind the registration without the family knowing. Run a [PPSR search](https://www.ppsr.gov.au/about-us/laws-rules-and-regulations/how-the-ppsr-protects-buyers-and-lessees) against the VIN before agreeing to a sale. If something is registered, the payout has to be dealt with as part of the sale; our guide to [selling a car with finance owing](/selling-a-car-with-finance-owing-queensland) covers how.

If the car is not going back on the road, the registration can be cancelled and any refund of unused registration goes to the registered owner. That is another reason to keep the owner's own bank details in the loop rather than yours.

## Cars that have been sitting

A car sold for someone else has often not been driven for a while. It might be in a unit basement in Surfers Paradise with a low clearance at the ramp, or on a narrow street in Burleigh Heads, or on a Mermaid Waters driveway with a flat battery and expired registration. That does not stop the sale, but it decides how the car leaves. An unregistered car should not be driven to the buyer just because it is only a few kilometres down the road. Tell whoever is quoting the parking situation, the clearance height and whether the car rolls and steers, so the right truck turns up.

Value works the way it does for any car: make, model, year, condition, completeness and salvage demand. Who is signing does not change it.

## What to have ready before selling a car on behalf of someone else

1. The registration certificate or number, and the VIN.
2. The authority that applies: TMR's authority to represent form, the enduring or general power of attorney, the QCAT order, or the business's written authority.
3. Evidence of identity for the person signing, plus whatever the authority form requires for the owner.
4. A PPSR result for the VIN, and a payout letter if finance is owing.
5. A safety certificate, or a decision on which exempt sale path you are using.
6. A decision on what happens to the plates, especially if they are personalised.
7. A signed receipt recording the buyer, seller, date, amount, VIN and the capacity you signed in.

A buyer who asks to see your authority is doing the owner a favour. A buyer who waves it away is the one to worry about.
