# Selling a NSW registered car in Queensland: which state's paperwork you lodge

<PostDetails slug="selling-nsw-registered-car-queensland" />

You deal with New South Wales, not TMR. When you are selling a NSW registered car in Queensland, the registration record belongs to Transport for NSW, so the notice that takes your name off the car is a NSW notice, the plates are NSW plates, and any refund of unused registration comes from NSW. Queensland only enters the picture when the car stays in Queensland and someone has to register it here.

That catches out plenty of sellers on the southern Gold Coast, where the border runs between Coolangatta and Tweed Heads and a car registered in one state is routinely garaged in the other. It also catches people who moved up the M1 a while ago and never switched the registration over.

**This guide is general information, not legal advice.** Registration rules, forms and fees in both states change. Check the NSW Government's [selling a used car guidance](https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/buying-and-selling-vehicles/selling-a-used-car) and TMR's page on [moving a vehicle to or from Queensland](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/transfer/interstate) before you lodge anything.

## Which situation are you in?

- **The NSW registration is current and the car is being sold to keep driving:** you lodge a NSW notice of disposal. The buyer transfers the NSW registration or registers the car in Queensland.
- **You moved to the Gold Coast with the car and never re-registered it:** read the section on the 14-day rule before you drive it to a buyer.
- **The car is going to a wrecker or for parts:** the NSW registration usually needs cancelling rather than transferring, and the plates go back to NSW.
- **The NSW registration has already expired:** the car is unregistered, and it has to be sold and moved as an unregistered car.
- **There is finance on the car:** that is a national question, not a state one. The same PPSR search applies whichever state registered the car.

## The notice of disposal is a NSW form

The **notice of disposal** is the NSW equivalent of telling TMR you have sold a car. It is the step that stops you being responsible for tolls and fines the next driver runs up. The NSW Government says you must lodge it within 14 days of the sale, and a late fee may apply after that.

Where you live does not change which state takes the notice. What changes is how you lodge it. Service NSW's [notice of disposal page](https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/transaction/submit-a-notice-of-disposal-for-a-vehicle) says a sale to a person or dealership located interstate needs to be lodged in person rather than through the online notice, and it offers a paper Notice of Disposal form for cases the online service does not cover. The form can be posted to Transport for NSW or handed in at a Service NSW centre. There are no Service NSW centres in Queensland, so from Southport or Robina the post is usually simpler than the drive south.

Allow for that when you agree a handover date. Posting a form takes longer than tapping through an online notice, and the 14 days run from the date of sale, not the date the paperwork arrives. Keep a copy of the completed form, the proof of postage and a signed receipt showing the buyer, the date, the price and the VIN.

## What the Queensland buyer has to do

The buyer's half is where the two states meet. TMR says a vehicle registered in another state that will be garaged at a Queensland address has to be registered in Queensland within 14 days, and the owner may be fined if it is not. After that, TMR treats a car that is still on interstate registration but garaged here as **unregistered in Queensland**.

To register it here, TMR says the owner hands in the interstate number plates and provides a current Queensland safety certificate at a transport and motoring customer service centre. That is the buyer's job, not yours, but it shapes the sale. A private buyer on the Gold Coast is effectively buying a car that needs a Queensland inspection and a trip to TMR within a fortnight, and many will price that in or ask you to arrange the certificate first.

A NSW **pink slip**, the safety inspection report NSW uses at renewal, is not a Queensland safety certificate. Do not promise a Queensland buyer that one stands in for the other. Queensland's [safety-certificate rules](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/roadworthy) set out who can issue the certificate and how long it lasts. If you are unsure whether your own sale needs one, ask TMR before you accept an offer, and read our guide on [selling a car without a roadworthy in Queensland](/sell-car-without-roadworthy-qld) for the Queensland-registered rules it is often confused with.

## If you moved here and never switched the registration

This is the case that needs care. The same TMR rule applies to you as the owner: a car kept at your Gold Coast address for more than 14 days on NSW registration is treated as unregistered in Queensland, and TMR says you can be fined.

That does not stop you selling it. It does change how you should get it to a buyer. Driving a car that Queensland treats as unregistered on the M1 to meet someone in Nerang is a risk you do not need to take. If it cannot be lawfully driven, have it towed or collected, or look at whether an [unregistered vehicle permit](/unregistered-vehicle-permit-queensland) fits the trip.

## Scrapping a NSW registered car on the Gold Coast

When the car is finished, the registration usually ends with it. The NSW Government's [cancelling registration guidance](https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/vehicle-registration/transferring-or-cancelling-registration/cancelling-your-registration) covers cancelling and returning the plates. Service NSW also says you do not lodge a notice of disposal but must cancel the registration when the car has been written off, or when you sell it to a wrecker without its number plates.

From Queensland you use the NSW [cancelling registration from interstate](https://www.nsw.gov.au/driving-boating-and-transport/vehicle-registration/transferring-or-cancelling-registration/cancelling-registration-from-interstate) process. You complete the refund form and either email it with the receipt for plates you have already surrendered, or post the form, your documents and the NSW plates to the address on the form. The refund is a pro-rata share of the unused motor vehicle tax from the date the plates are surrendered. The registration fee itself is not refunded, and you must cancel before the registration expires to get anything back.

The order matters for the same reason it does in Queensland: cancel first and the refund is yours; let the registration run out and it is gone. Our [Queensland cancellation checklist](/cancel-car-registration-queensland-after-sale) walks through the TMR version, which is the one you would use once the car carries Queensland plates.

## Plates, finance and write-offs follow their own rules

**Personalised NSW plates** are a NSW product. TMR's interstate guidance says owners with personalised or customised plates from another state need to check with that state's authority about keeping them. Sort that out before the plates go in an envelope to Dubbo.

**Finance** is recorded nationally. A security interest over the car sits on the [Personal Property Securities Register](https://www.ppsr.gov.au/) whether the car is registered in NSW or Queensland, and it has to be paid out or released before a buyer takes clear title. Our guide to [selling a car with finance owing](/selling-a-car-with-finance-owing-queensland) covers the payout letter and the PPSR search.

**A written-off car** carries its written-off status across the border. If an insurer has written it off, check its status before you sell and read our explanation of [statutory and repairable write-offs](/statutory-vs-repairable-write-off-queensland).

## Checklist before the car leaves

1. Confirm the NSW registration status: current, expired or written off.
2. Decide whether the car is being sold to keep driving or going for parts or scrap.
3. Check the PPSR and get a payout letter if there is finance.
4. Remove any personalised plates you want to keep, following the NSW process.
5. Record the buyer, date, price and VIN in a signed receipt for both parties.
6. Lodge the NSW notice of disposal within 14 days, on paper if the buyer is in Queensland, or cancel the NSW registration and return the plates if the car is being scrapped.
7. Do not drive a car Queensland treats as unregistered; arrange transport instead.

When you ask for a vehicle-specific offer, tell us up front that the car is NSW registered, whether the registration is current, and where it is parked. We confirm what paperwork we need for the actual car before anyone is booked, but Service NSW, Transport for NSW and TMR remain the authorities for their own registrations.
