# How much is my scrap car worth on the Gold Coast?

The honest answer is that nobody can tell you over the internet. Any site that advertises a flat figure for "any car" is either quoting the floor and negotiating down on arrival, or quoting the ceiling to get the phone call. What we can do is explain exactly what the number is built from, so you can judge whether a quote you have been given is reasonable.

## A scrap car is priced as parts plus metal, not as a car

This is the single thing most owners get wrong. When you sell to a private buyer, they are paying for transport — something they intend to drive. When you sell to a dismantler, nobody is going to drive it again. The value is the sum of what can be recovered and resold, plus the weight of what is left.

That distinction explains most of the surprises. A car that will not start can still be worth a meaningful amount, because the engine was never the problem. And a car that drives perfectly can be worth less than the owner expects at a wrecker, because a wrecker is not paying for "drives perfectly".

## The five things that actually move the number

**1. Whether the car is complete.** This matters more than age, more than kilometres, and more than whether it runs. A complete car has an engine, a transmission, four wheels, glass, lights, panels, seats and a catalytic converter. Once parts have been sold off, each one comes off the offer. The gap between a complete car and a picked-over shell of the same model is usually several hundred dollars and can be more.

**2. Parts demand for that make and model.** Common cars on Queensland roads have a ready market for used parts, so their components are worth more to a dismantler than something rare with no buyers waiting. This is why two cars of identical weight and age can be quoted differently — it is not arbitrary.

**3. The catalytic converter.** Converters contain small quantities of platinum, palladium and rhodium, and their recovery value varies by model and with metal markets. A car with its converter already removed is worth noticeably less, and it is worth mentioning up front if yours has been cut out.

**4. Scrap steel price on the day.** Whatever cannot be reused is sold by weight into the metal market, and that market moves. It is a commodity like any other. A quote given six months ago is not a quote today, in either direction.

**5. Where the car is and how hard it is to reach.** A car on a flat driveway is a straightforward job. One in a basement with a clearance bar, boxed in behind two other vehicles, or on soft ground that will not hold a loaded truck takes more time and sometimes different equipment. This rarely changes a quote much, but it is the reason we ask.

## Why quotes differ between buyers

Different buyers recover different things. An operation that dismantles and sells parts can pay more for a complete car than one that only crushes, because it has more ways to make the money back. Someone who already has three of your model on the shelf will want yours less than someone who has none.

So getting two or three quotes is sensible. What is worth watching for is not the highest number but the firmest one — a high quote that drops when the truck arrives is worth less than a realistic quote that gets honoured.

## What to have ready to get an accurate figure

You will get a much better answer in one phone call if you can say:

- Make, model, year and rough kilometres
- Whether it starts, drives, or neither
- What is obviously wrong with it — engine, transmission, accident damage, rust, flood
- Whether anything has already been removed or sold
- Whether it is registered, and whether it is in your name
- Where it is sitting and whether a truck can get to it

That is enough for a firm number on most cars, usually inside a minute.

## When scrapping is the wrong answer

If your car is registered, roadworthy, reasonably modern and you are not in a hurry, sell it privately. You will get more, sometimes substantially more, because a private buyer is paying for a car they will drive. We say this on the phone regularly.

Scrapping wins when the repair bill is higher than the car is worth, when it will not pass a safety inspection, when it has not moved in years, or when you need it gone by a date and do not want the listing-and-no-shows process. Those are real situations and they are most of our work — but they are not every situation.

## The Gold Coast wrinkle: rust

Cars kept near the beach corrode from underneath, and owners routinely find out at a failed safety inspection rather than by looking. Structural rust ends a car's road life, but it does not touch the engine, transmission, converter or interior — so a beachside car that has just failed an inspection is very often worth more than its owner assumes at that moment.
