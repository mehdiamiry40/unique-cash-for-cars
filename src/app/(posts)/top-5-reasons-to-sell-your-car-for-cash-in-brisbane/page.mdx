# Five reasons to sell your car for cash in Brisbane

We buy cars, so treat this with appropriate suspicion. What follows is our honest read on when a cash sale is the better choice — including the case where it isn't.

## 1. It takes a day instead of a month

A private sale is a project. Clean the car, photograph it, write the listing, field messages, arrange inspections, wait for people who don't show up, negotiate, then handle the payment and transfer. Four to six weeks is normal for an ordinary car at a fair price.

A cash sale is a phone call and a truck. For people who are moving, have already bought the replacement, or simply don't want the project, that difference is the whole argument.

## 2. Condition stops mattering

Private buyers want a car that starts, drives and passes a roadworthy. The moment yours doesn't, your buyer pool collapses to bargain hunters and people who won't turn up.

Cash buyers price on what's recoverable, so a car that doesn't run isn't a problem — it's just a different number. No roadworthy certificate is needed when the car is being removed from the road rather than resold to a driver.

## 3. No roadworthy certificate, no safety certificate hassle

In Queensland, privately selling a registered vehicle generally requires a safety certificate. Getting one on an older car frequently surfaces work you weren't planning to pay for — tyres, brakes, suspension bushes — and you're spending that money to enable a sale, not to keep the car.

Selling to a licensed buyer who's removing the vehicle sidesteps that entirely.

## 4. The price doesn't move at the last minute

The worst part of a private sale is the driveway renegotiation: a buyer arrives, finds something, and knocks a few hundred off knowing you've already mentally sold the car.

A cash quote given on accurate information should be honoured on arrival. Ours is. If a buyer routinely drops the price when the truck turns up, that's a reason to use someone else — not a reason to avoid cash sales.

## 5. It's off your hands, properly

An unwanted car in a driveway isn't neutral. It's registration you may still be paying, a space you can't use, fluids slowly leaking onto the ground, and a vehicle losing recoverable value every month. Getting it gone has a value that doesn't show up in the sale price.

## The honest counterpoint

If your car is registered, roadworthy, reasonably modern and you're not in a hurry, **sell it privately.** You will get more money — often substantially more — because a private buyer is paying for a car they intend to drive, while we're paying for the parts and materials we can recover.

The cash route wins on speed, certainty and hassle. It does not win on price for a good car. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.
