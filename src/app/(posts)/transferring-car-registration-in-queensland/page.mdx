# Transferring car registration in Queensland

Most of the delays we see at pickup are paperwork, not mechanical. None of it is complicated, but it does have an order, and the order matters more when the car is unregistered, inherited, or not in the seller's name.

**Requirements and forms change.** Treat this as an outline of the shape of the process and confirm the current detail with the Queensland Department of Transport and Main Roads before you rely on it.

## The two halves of a transfer

A Queensland registration transfer has a seller side and a buyer side, and they are separate obligations. Doing your half does not complete the other.

**The seller** notifies TMR that the vehicle has been disposed of. This is what stops you being the registered operator — which is what matters if the car later collects tolls, parking fines or an infringement. Lodge it promptly; Queensland works to a short window after the sale, and the protection only starts when the notice is in.

**The buyer** lodges the transfer application and pays the duty and transfer fee. Until they do, the registration record still points at the previous owner.

The practical lesson: never rely on a buyer to "sort it out". Lodge your own notice of disposal, keep proof you did, and keep a copy of everything you signed.

## Do you need a safety certificate?

For a **private sale of a registered vehicle**, Queensland generally requires a safety certificate — often still called a roadworthy — provided by the seller.

There are situations where one is not required, and the two that come up most in our work are:

- The vehicle is **unregistered**, and stays unregistered through the sale.
- The vehicle is being **sold for dismantling or scrap** and is not going back on the road.

There are other exemptions, including some transfers between family members and some dealer transactions. Because the exemptions are specific, check your circumstances rather than assuming — it is a short call to TMR and it settles the question.

This is the reason selling a failed, rusted or non-running car to a dismantler is administratively simpler than selling it privately. There is nothing to certify because nothing is going back on the road.

## What to have ready

For an ordinary sale:

- **Photo ID** matching the registered owner
- The **registration certificate**, or the registration number if you cannot find it
- A **safety certificate**, if the sale requires one
- A **payout letter** if there is finance owing — see [selling a car with finance owing](/selling-a-car-with-finance-owing-queensland)
- The **keys**, if they exist

## Number plates and refunds

Plates and registration are handled separately from the sale itself, and the right move depends on what happens to the car.

If the car stays on the road with a new owner, the plates ordinarily go with it. If the registration is being cancelled — which is what happens when a car is scrapped or dismantled — cancelling with TMR is what releases any refund of the unused portion, and the plates are dealt with as part of that process. Personalised plates are their own case and are usually retained by the seller.

If the registration still has real time left on it, do not skip this. It is genuinely money, and it is straightforward to claim.

## The three situations that actually cause trouble

**The car is not in your name.** A previous owner never completed their side, so the record still shows them. You cannot transfer what you are not recorded as owning. This is fixable, but not at the roadside on collection day, and it usually needs the previous owner's cooperation. Deal with it before booking anything.

**The owner has died.** An estate vehicle needs evidence of your authority to deal with it — typically the death certificate plus documentation showing you are the executor or administrator, along with your own ID. Requirements vary with the size and structure of the estate. Ring ahead.

**The car was left behind by a tenant.** Queensland has a defined process for goods abandoned at the end of a tenancy, and it has to have been followed before anyone can lawfully sell or remove the vehicle. Being the property owner or the agent is not by itself enough. We handle these regularly and the paperwork is the whole job.

## When the car is leaving the road permanently

If the vehicle is being dismantled, the transfer is simpler than a normal sale: no safety certificate, no new registered operator, and the registration is cancelled rather than transferred. What still matters is proving who you are and that the car is yours to sell — that requirement does not relax, and a buyer who does not ask for it is not one to use.
