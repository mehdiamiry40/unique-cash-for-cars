# Where do old junk cars go on the Gold Coast?

A car leaves your driveway on a tilt tray and that's usually the last you think about it. What happens next determines how much it was worth to you, so it's worth understanding.

## The four destinations

**Dismantlers and wreckers.** The car is stripped systematically: fluids drained and contained, battery removed, catalytic converter recovered, then the drivetrain, panels, glass, wheels, lights and interior components pulled and catalogued for resale. What's left — the bare shell — goes to a metal recycler. This route recovers the most value, which is why dismantlers generally pay the most for complete cars.

**Metal recyclers.** The car is depolluted, then crushed or shredded, and the steel and aluminium go back into the supply chain. This is the destination for shells that have already been stripped, or cars so far gone there's nothing worth removing. Payment is essentially by weight, so it's the lowest-value option.

**Auction.** Insurance write-offs and repossessions often go through salvage auctions, where they're bought by repairers, exporters and rebuilders. Worth more than scrap if the car is repairable, but slow and with fees attached.

**Export.** Some models — particularly certain Japanese and European vehicles with strong overseas parts demand — are worth more shipped than dismantled locally. This is a narrow category and mostly applies to specific engines and drivetrains.

## Why "junk" is usually the wrong word

The car sitting in your yard that hasn't started since 2019 is not junk in any meaningful sense. It's a few hundred kilos of recyclable steel with several thousand dollars of manufactured components bolted to it, some of which have a ready market.

The parts that hold value regardless of whether the car runs:

- Engine and transmission, even seized or blown — cores have value
- Catalytic converter, which contains platinum, palladium and rhodium
- Alternator, starter motor, radiator, air conditioning compressor
- Panels, doors, bonnet, tailgate — anything straight and unrusted
- Glass, lights, mirrors, wheels
- Interior modules, seats, dashboard components

A car that has already been picked over for these is worth close to scrap value. A complete one is worth considerably more, which is why the first question any buyer asks is whether the car is intact.

## What this means for how you sell

If your car is complete, sell it to someone who dismantles — they can pay more because they recover more. If it's already been stripped, you're selling steel and should expect steel prices.

Either way, the practical thing that costs people money is delay. A complete car left outdoors for another two years is a less complete car: the interior degrades, the wiring gets chewed, panels rust through, and fluids leak into the ground, which is its own problem.

## Doing it properly

Queensland has rules about how end-of-life vehicles are depolluted and disposed of, and for good reason — engine oil, coolant, brake fluid, fuel and battery acid all cause real damage if they end up in stormwater. A legitimate operator drains and contains all of it before anything is crushed.

It's worth asking whoever collects your car what happens to it. If they can't tell you, that's informative.
