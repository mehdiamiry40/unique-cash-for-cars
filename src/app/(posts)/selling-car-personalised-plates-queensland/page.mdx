# Selling a car with personalised plates in Queensland: keep, transfer or cancel

<PostDetails slug="selling-car-personalised-plates-queensland" />

The plates do not have to go with the car, and usually they should not go by accident. When you are selling a car with personalised plates in Queensland, the plates are treated separately from the vehicle. **Personalised plates** belong to the plate owner, so you choose one of three things before the sale: take them off and keep them, transfer them to the buyer on purpose, or cancel them.

The choice matters in practice as well. TMR does not let you transfer a registration online while personalised or customised plates are attached, so a sale that leaves them bolted on usually means an unplanned trip to a customer service centre for both parties.

**This guide is general information, not legal advice.** Plate rules, forms and fees change. Check TMR's current [personalised number plate guidance](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/numberplate/personalised-plates) before you lodge anything.

## Selling a car with personalised plates: which situation are you in?

- **Keeping the plates, selling the car registered:** remove the plates before the transfer and put standard plates on the car.
- **Buyer wants the plates too:** transfer the plates to them separately, with both of you signing the plate transfer form.
- **Car going for scrap or parts:** cancel the vehicle's registration, and keep the personalised plates unless you also want the combination cancelled.
- **You no longer want the plates at all:** cancel them with a separate form and hand them in.
- **The plates are customised, not personalised:** different rules. They cannot be sold, only moved to another vehicle you own or left to become standard plates.

If you are not sure whether your plates are personalised or customised, confirm it with TMR before you plan the sale. The difference decides whether the plates can be sold at all.

## Keeping your plates when you sell the car

This is the most common case, and the step people miss is timing. Removing personalised plates is a TMR transaction, not just a screwdriver job. You lodge a [remove and/or attach personalised/customised number plates application (F2964)](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/numberplate/personalised-plates/attaching) at a transport and motoring customer service centre, with:

- evidence of identity for one registered operator of the vehicle and for every plate owner
- the form signed by one registered operator and all plate owners
- the standard plate fee

You leave with a set of **standard plates** for the car, and TMR requires them to be attached within one day. Once the car is wearing standard plates, the plate restriction on online transfers no longer applies, and the buyer receives the car on ordinary plates.

Do this before the car changes hands. A buyer who drives off on your personalised plates has a car whose registration cannot be transferred online, and you are left arranging a counter visit with someone who has already paid. Our [Queensland registration transfer guide](/transferring-car-registration-in-queensland) covers the rest of the seller and buyer steps once the plates are sorted.

Keep the removed plates somewhere you will find them. When you want them on your next car, the same F2964 form handles the attach step, and the plates must go on that vehicle within one day of lodging it.

## Selling the plates with the car

Some buyers want the plate as much as the vehicle. That is allowed, but it is two transfers, not one. The registration moves the usual way, and the plates move under a [personalised number plate/s transfer application (F2963)](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/numberplate/personalised-plates/buying) signed by every seller (the **disposer**) and every buyer (the **acquirer**).

Because the plates are still attached when the car changes hands, this path cannot be finished online. Plan for both of you to deal with a customer service centre, and agree beforehand who lodges what and who pays the plate transfer fee.

Price the plates separately in your sale receipt. That keeps the record clear if the buyer later questions what they paid for, and it stops the plates quietly disappearing into the car's price.

The fee may not apply when personalised plates are given to a spouse, parent or child of the disposer, or pass to a beneficiary, administrator or executor of a deceased estate. If the car belonged to someone who has died, read our [deceased estate car sale guide](/deceased-estate-car-sale-queensland) first. TMR places a restriction on the person's plates as well as the vehicle, which changes the order of steps.

## Scrapping or wrecking a car with personalised plates

A car going to a dismantler or recycler is normally sold unregistered, which means its registration is cancelled first. TMR's [registration cancellation guidance](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/cancel) says you do not need to return the plates if you are keeping personalised or customised plates. General-issue plates are surrendered; yours are not.

So the practical rule is simple: cancel the registration, keep your plates, and make sure they are off the car before it is collected. If the car is being winched off a driveway in Nerang or out of a unit car park in Surfers Paradise, unbolt the plates before the tow truck arrives. Plates left in the boot, or still on the car, leave with whoever takes the car.

A car with plates still attached is also not eligible for an unregistered vehicle permit, which matters if you were planning to drive it to a buyer. See our [unregistered vehicle permit guide](/unregistered-vehicle-permit-queensland) for when a permit works and when towing is simpler, and our checklist for [cancelling Queensland registration after a sale](/cancel-car-registration-queensland-after-sale) for the refund side.

## Cancelling personalised plates you no longer want

Keeping the plate combination and cancelling it are different transactions. To cancel personalised plates, attached to a vehicle or not, lodge a [cancel personalised/customised plate application (F5339)](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/numberplate/personalised-plates/attaching) signed by all plate owners, and hand the plates in at the same time.

If you are cancelling the vehicle's registration as well, TMR takes both forms together and the plates are surrendered with them. Do not assume the vehicle cancellation takes care of the plates. The plate cancellation is its own form, and without it you still hold the plates.

Before cancelling, consider whether the combination has value to someone else. Personalised plates can be transferred to another person, so selling them may be an option that cancelling closes off.

## Customised plates follow different rules

**Customised plates** cannot be sold, because their ownership is not transferable. According to TMR's [customised plate guidance](https://www.qld.gov.au/transport/registration/numberplate/customised-plates/about), when a car wearing them is sold the seller can either:

- remove them and attach them to another vehicle owned by the same person or organisation, or
- leave them on the car, in which case they become standard plates when the vehicle is transferred.

The online-transfer restriction applies to customised plates too. If you want to keep them, remove them through TMR before the sale, as with personalised plates.

## A short checklist before the car leaves

1. Confirm whether the plates are personalised, customised or general issue.
2. Decide: keep, transfer to the buyer, or cancel.
3. If keeping, lodge F2964, fit the standard plates within one day, then transfer or cancel the registration.
4. If transferring the plates, lodge F2963 signed by everyone on both sides, alongside the registration transfer.
5. If scrapping, cancel the registration and physically remove your plates before collection.
6. Tell the buyer or collector the plate plan before the pickup is booked.
7. Record the plate outcome on the sale receipt and keep TMR's confirmations.
